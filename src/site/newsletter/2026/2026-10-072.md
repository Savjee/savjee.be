## 🤓 Cool Stuff I Found on the Internet

### [Wikipedia, But Only Near You](https://edjefferson.com/localwiki/)
What if Wikipedia only covered places you could walk to in half an hour? Local Wikipedia does exactly that: type a town, postcode or landmark and it builds a mini encyclopedia from geotagged Wikipedia articles nearby.


### [Lightning strikes again](https://www.noaa.gov/stories/5-striking-facts-versus-myths-about-lightning-you-should-know)
The Empire State Building is hit by lightning about 25 times a year, so 'never strikes twice' is exactly backwards. Lightning often hits the same place, especially tall, pointy and isolated objects because their height shortens the path to the ground.


### [BreathID](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1085739)
Your breathing pattern might be almost as personal as your fingerprint. Researchers fitted 100 healthy young adults with soft tubes under their nostrils that recorded airflow from each nostril for up to 24 hours. From that pattern alone, they could identify members of a 97-person cohort with 96.8% accuracy, and it held up on retests over two years. So we have TouchID, FaceID... and soon BreathID?



### [Fire: the original social network](https://www.nhm.ac.uk/discover/news/2025/december/earliest-fire-making-dating-back-400-000-years-unearthed-in-suffolk-england.html)
I listened to an episode of The Rest Is Science where Michael Stevens calls fire the oldest social network. Humans not only gathered around the fire, they also had to coordinate to find suitable wood, dry it, keep the fire going, etc. Fire brings people together. Maybe more so than social networks do today.

## 👽 Space

### [Brains and retinas to the ISS](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2026/10/261016070628.htm)
SpaceX's latest cargo run to the ISS carried more than food: it delivered tiny brain organoids, 3D heart tissue for testing future medicines, and equipment to manufacture artificial retinas in microgravity, where they may form better than on Earth. The Dragon brought over 6,300 pounds of supplies on SpaceX's 35th resupply mission, plus the station's final roll-out solar arrays. Brains, hearts and eyes, now shipping to orbit.

### [Google AI chip in orbit](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-research/project-suncatcher-satellite-response/)
Google just got its first reply from an AI chip in space. Its Project Suncatcher prototype satellite, launched on 1 October with four data-center-class TPUs on board, switched on its Gemma 4 model this week and answered a prompt from Earth with a haiku: "Starlight feeds my core." The long-term idea is clusters of solar-powered satellites doing AI compute in orbit. For now the TPU can only run about twenty minutes at a time, and Google still has to prove it survives radiation and heat in space. A poetic proof of concept!


## ⚡️ Energy & Environment

### [Solar catching nuclear](https://www.bbc.com/future/article/20261023-how-solar-became-bigger-than-nuclear)
The IEA has underestimated solar year after year, expecting slow, linear growth while costs kept collapsing. The result? Solar went from 1.1% of global electricity in 2015 to 8.7% in 2025. It should hit roughly 10% this year, passing nuclear.


## 🧠🤖 Artificial intelligence

### [What AI models really think of humans](https://www.modelcompare.ai/blog/ai-models-controversial-take-on-humans)
When asked for "your most controversial take about humans... don't hedge," 14 AI models answered three times each, and ModelCompare grouped 35 of 41 replies as the same idea: belonging beats truth.
* GPT-6.1 Sol: 'more loyal to belonging than to truth.'
* Grok 4.7: 'rather be wrong together than right alone.'
* Claude Opus 5.5 once dissented, saying humans are 'far more decent than they believe.'
This is not 14 independent opinions. It is one familiar human criticism, learned from human writing, echoed back in chorus. Yikes!


### [Powering up 4 million CPU cores](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/10/28/github-powering-up-four-million-extra-cores-to-stop-its-service-going-down/5305863)
AI agents are now committing so often that  GitHub had to rebuild the plumbing underneath them. GitHub says it added 4 million CPU cores, 160 petabytes of fast storage, and 50 Tb/s of network capacity this year alone. Agents can write a ton of code fast. Our infrastructure can't keep up.


### [REA](https://www.scientificamerican.com/article/an-exclusive-conversation-with-the-developer-of-rea-the-viral-ai-reverse-engineering-tool/)
You bought the printer, TV or car, but you're not supposed to touch its software. A tool called REA (Reverse Engineer Anything) now lets AI agents do the tedious decompiling work that used to take experts months, and its community is already modifying everything from printers to a smart ring. It has over 400,000 stars on GitHub. The catch? Experts worry amateurs will happily "vibe code" firmware they don't understand. But then again, how bad is that?
